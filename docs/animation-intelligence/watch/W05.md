## 1. The frame budget — the reason this list is split into ten sessions

Frames are images in your context and each one costs. Ten videos is the budget of one session at the
detail written next to each video:

- `balanced` — scene-aware frames, cap 100. For tutorials that SHOW motion. Read every frame; that is
  what the small batch buys. If a video yields more than 80 frames, re-run it with `--max-frames 60`.
- `efficient` — up to 50 keyframes. For long videos or ones that are mostly words with some visuals.
- `transcript` — no frames. For talks and analyses. If the transcript points at a demo, frame only
  those minutes: `--start MM:SS --end MM:SS --detail efficient`.

Watch one video at a time and write its entries before starting the next. When the context gets
heavy, stop: tick what you finished in your notes file, commit, and say which video is next. The
next session runs this same prompt and continues from the first unticked video.

## 3. What to write, per video (the deliverable)

1. **Knowledge entries** — one JSON file per technique or rule the video teaches (usually one to
   four per video) at `docs/animation-intelligence/knowledge/inbox/W05-<video-number>-<slug>.json`,
   in Part 25's exact twenty-field shape (the loader refuses anything else):

   ```
   concept, category, definition, purpose, visible_effect, psychological_or_gameplay_effect, physical_interpretation, use_cases, non_use_cases, interactions, style_variations, cadence_representation, control_surface, detection_and_measurement_methods, generation_or_modification_methods, failure_modes, roblox_considerations, performance_implications, examples, evidence_status
   ```

   `category` is one of `essential`, `advanced`, `optional`, `specialized`, `experimental`. Every
   field is present and specific; write `"not applicable"` rather than leaving one empty.
   `evidence_status` names the source exactly: `"video: <url> @ MM:SS — <what was shown or said>;
   experimental until validated"`. `detection_and_measurement_methods` names what `ai/motion.js`
   could measure (velocity, acceleration, jerk, curvature, contact drift, chain lead/lag, key density)
   or says `"not measurable in this build"` — never invent a measurement. If the MCP tool
   `propose_knowledge_entry` exists (the learning loop has been built), call it instead of writing
   the file: it validates the shape and files the entry.
2. **Capture candidates** — if the video SHOWS a motion worth having as a measured reference (a
   clean walk, a heavy attack, a jump and landing, a hit reaction), add a line to your notes: URL,
   start–end timestamps, action type, why. The user captures it later with Roblox Studio's
   Animation Capture; you do not.
3. **Checks the review or planner should enforce** — a line in your notes of the form
   `check: <name> — <what to measure> — <threshold or convention> — <source video @ time>`. These
   become measured checks in a later session; a check with no measurable quantity is a note, not a
   check — say which.
4. **Your notes file** `docs/animation-intelligence/watch/W05-notes.md`: a copy of the ten-video list at
   the top with ticks, then per video one dated paragraph in your own words — specific (frame
   counts, ratios, the order in which body parts move, the exact rule of thumb), what surprised you,
   what contradicted an existing knowledge card (name the card), and the entries you wrote.
