Watched at `transcript` only (1131 caption segments), deliberately — a single continuous live-build screen recording where every technique is narrated with exact property names and values as it happens; no frame pass was needed to confirm any of the six entries below, all of which quote the transcript's own precise language. Six entries: **phase-offset dual emitters for a persistent glow** (two identical low-rate emitters started half a lifetime apart so their fade cycles sum to roughly flat — the single most sophisticated technique in the video, and a clean case where Cadence's VFXSpec, one emitter per compiled effect, has no field for "a second copy of this at a declared phase offset"; that composition is PNX's job, not VFXSpec's, by the same boundary decision already in the code); **concurrent particle budget = rate × lifetime** (a high rate is not itself spammy if lifetime is short — directly legible against Cadence's own `EFFECT_ROLES` budget-share caps, though VFXSpec does not expose rate/lifetime as separate dials to multiply); **simultaneous-activation baseline defeats authoring-order phase drift** (emitters toggled on at different times while building drift out of phase purely by accident; disable-and-re-enable-together before judging the result, since the real game always activates everything at once — and Cadence's declared, frame-numbered `timing.event` is structurally immune to this exact class of bug, worth recording as a case where the authored/keyframe-native model sidesteps a live-toggle workflow's own failure mode); **per-instance randomization breaks a unison look** (the comma min,max convention on rotation/speed/lifetime, named as a cheap, high-value fix); **a particle must fade at both lifetime extremes or it pops** (near-universal, presenter says he sees it violated constantly); and **a speed floor of 0.001, never a literal 0**, to dodge an orientation-mode clipping bug the presenter can trigger reliably but cannot fully explain.

What surprised me: the phase-offset glow trick is the first technique in this whole watchlist that is a genuine COMPOSITION problem (two synchronized-but-offset emitters) rather than a single-emitter authoring choice — it lands exactly on the VFXSpec/PNX boundary the codebase already draws for other reasons (layers, multi-node graphs), which W08's own throughline flagged from a different angle (Cadence's two systems not always agreeing on scope). Also notable: the simultaneous-activation entry is a rare case of Cadence's data model being *structurally better* than the live-authoring workflow being taught, purely as a side effect of storing timing as declared frames rather than wall-clock toggle history — worth keeping in mind as a positive selling point, not just a gap list.
